## Four limits printed on the product page

The price list is the easy part. These four lines sit in the product description and each one changes what you do after checkout.

**The mailbox is temporary.** “The mailbox is a temporary mailbox, please change the mailbox in time after delivery.” You receive the account number, the password, the bound mailbox address and that mailbox’s password. The mailbox itself won’t last, so the first job is moving the account onto an email address you control.

**The account arrives unbound, with the token off.** Binding a phone number is the step that unlocks changing the mailbox and changing the password. That means the security setup is your homework, not something included in the $2.37.

**Friend requests need a purchase first.** A new account has to spend at least 30 yuan on Steam before the add-friend function opens. The same paragraph then gives the threshold as “more than $30”, so the page contradicts itself on the amount. Whichever figure applies to you, it is real money on top of the account price, and it is the item buyers most often discover after delivery.

**The three-day after-sales window covers quality problems, not second thoughts.** The policy section reads: “Account products: 3-day after-sale period from the date of purchase; free replacement for quality issues.” A separate line higher up the same page reads: “Once shipped, refunds are not supported (except for the secret account problem).” Both statements are on the Steam page. The practical reading is that you get a three-day window to report a defective account and receive a replacement, and no refund for a working account you no longer want.

One operational note from the page: if the Steam store fails to load or throws an error code, start your accelerator before launching Steam.

## Who should register a free Steam account instead

Skip the purchase if any of these describe you:

- You want a main account you will keep for years and attach a payment method to.
- You want account history, playtime, or a game library. These are blank accounts.
- You can’t accept that a working account is non-refundable after delivery.
- You can’t get to a computer and rebind the email within three days of receiving it.
- You need friend requests to work immediately, without an extra Steam purchase.

A free account you register yourself costs nothing, carries no third-party risk, and has none of the mailbox or binding chores. Buying an account makes sense for a narrow set of cases: you want a second account for a specific region, you need one quickly, or you’re setting up something disposable and don’t want to attach your own identity to it.
